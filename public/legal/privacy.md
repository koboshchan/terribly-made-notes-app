Notes Privacy Policy

Draft for review, not yet effective. Updated October 15, 2026. This draft describes reviewed source code, not a certification of the live deployment; encryption and deletion safeguards must be verified in production before this notice takes effect.

Lucas Zhang, also known as kobosh, operates Notes at notes.kobosh.com and its iOS companion as a minor-run hobby project in British Columbia, Canada. Lucas is responsible for privacy questions and requests at lz@kjt.lol.

Clerk manages sign-in and processes the account information you provide, linked sign-in information if you choose that method, and session and device information for authentication. The Notes database associates your content with a Clerk user identifier and stores registration time and an administrator flag where applicable. Notes does not store your Clerk password.

Notes receives audio you upload or record, filenames, selected language, and audio metadata such as duration, format, bitrate, sample rate, channel count, and recording time when available. It stores transcripts, generated titles and descriptions, study notes, flashcards, quiz questions, class names and descriptions, class assignments, upload fingerprints and retry identifiers, processing preferences and outcomes, processing status, errors, timestamps, and sharing records. Shortcut upload features store token digests, prefixes, names, expiry and usage metadata associated with the account; generated secrets are shown when created.

Chat requests include your messages, the recent history sent by the client, and relevant note content. The current main-branch web chat keeps its conversation in page memory rather than a persistent chat database. This does not prevent an AI provider or error log from retaining request information. Support messages include whatever you choose to send the operator.

Audio is normalized and sent to the configured speech-to-text endpoint. The operator states that speech-to-text, and any text-to-speech, runs locally on hardware the operator controls and that this data is never used to train models. Transcripts, class names used for classification, and generated note content are sent to the language-model endpoint to generate summaries and study materials, and chat sends note context and conversation text. The operator states that this endpoint is the paid Google Gemini API. That is the operator's description of the deployment, not a verified fact from this review; the code lets the operator change endpoints.

Text-to-speech provider settings exist, but the reviewed web and iOS code does not implement an in-app request or player using that provider. If a speech-generation feature is introduced, this notice must describe which text is sent and to whom before that feature is used.

Google's published Gemini API terms for paid usage state that prompts and outputs are not used to train its models, and that Google retains prompts and outputs for up to 55 days for abuse monitoring and legal disclosure, with flagged content potentially reviewed by authorized Google employees (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/usage-policies). Those terms are Google's and may change. The operator must still confirm the billing tier actually in use, any other upstream providers, and processing locations before publication. You may ask lz@kjt.lol about the active providers before uploading. Notes cannot promise that any provider discards requests sooner than its own terms allow.

The operator can access server records and files when administering the service, providing support, or investigating errors. Shared content is also accessible to recipients. Information may be disclosed where required by law or reasonably necessary to protect security or legal rights.

Notes uses information to authenticate you, process audio, save and organize notes, generate study materials, answer chat requests, provide sharing and exports, diagnose failures, protect the service, and handle support or privacy requests. The reviewed application has no advertising, payment, or third-party analytics integration and no feature that sells personal information. Notes does not train its own model on your notes, but this does not govern the AI providers described above.

The backend stores records in MongoDB and uploaded audio and generated files in a server data directory. The reviewed server code encrypts these files using per-account keys wrapped by a server-side master key configured through FILE_ENCRYPTION_MASTER_KEY. This is not end-to-end encryption: the running server can decrypt files, MongoDB records are not encrypted by this file-encryption feature, and temporary plaintext files are used during upload and media processing. This does not establish that an older deployed version, legacy files, logs, or backups are encrypted; existing plaintext data needs migration and verification before such a claim can apply to production. The operator and infrastructure may process IP addresses, request paths, timestamps, browser or device details, and operational errors. Logs can include generated content or provider response text when processing fails. Hosting and network operators and AI providers may process data outside Canada, where authorities may have lawful access. The deployed infrastructure operators, storage locations, and log retention must be confirmed before publication.

Clerk uses cookies and related storage for authentication and security. Blocking these may prevent sign-in. The reviewed main-branch web application has no separate advertising cookies or analytics tracker.

On iOS, microphone access is requested for recording. Imported audio is accessed when you select or share it. The app saves recordings and caches note lists, classes, and note details on the device for offline access. The reviewed iOS upload extension uses an authentication token stored in shared app-group preferences. Server deletion does not automatically clear every device cache, recording, export, or device backup. Other app versions may use different local storage safeguards.

Sharing creates a secret URL that anyone possessing it can open without signing in. Depending on the share type and client, it exposes the shared note's title, description, content and creation time; the reviewed public APIs do not expose the original audio or full private note record. Public chat, if you turn it on for a share, can send shared content and a visitor's conversation to an AI provider. It is disabled unless enabled for that link. Public-chat abuse controls store short-lived hashed usage buckets derived from a share token, its owner, and, if a trusted proxy header is configured, a visitor IP address; an unconfigured client identity uses a shared unknown bucket. These counters are scheduled for database expiry, not retained chat messages. Each share expires automatically after a period set by the operator, from 1 to 365 days (30 days by default), or never if the operator chooses; you cannot choose the expiry yourself. Available web controls let you disable a note link or delete a shared set; app-version support can differ. Disabling an individual note link does not revoke a separate shared-set link containing that note. Deleting the note stops it being returned through either type of link. Recipients may already have copies, so do not put sensitive content in a shared note.

The processing worker attempts to delete the original upload after successful study-material generation. Converted audio, transcript files, generated files, and database records remain until you delete them or your account is deleted. Failed or interrupted jobs may retain the original for retry. There is no general automatic expiry for saved notes.

Deleting a note removes its active database record and associated server directory in the normal deletion flow. Shared-set metadata may remain. The deletion flow requests cancellation of active processing; a failure can require retry or manual cleanup. The authenticated account-deletion endpoint first destroys the account file key, requests deletion of the Clerk identity, and attempts local cleanup. It can report pending cleanup or identity deletion and require retry or operator help. If an account is deleted through Clerk instead, or to repeat cleanup, the configured signed user.deleted webhook first destroys the account file key, then requests processing cancellation and removes owned database records, job records and stored files. The handler returns a failure response if cleanup is incomplete so the webhook service can retry. Delivery, retry limits, and failure monitoring must be verified by the operator; cleanup is not guaranteed to finish automatically. Destroyed-key markers and encryption nonce records are intentionally retained to prevent key reuse; logs, anonymous usage counters, and records not identifiable as owned by the account are outside this account cleanup. Contact lz@kjt.lol if you want verification or if cleanup seems incomplete.

Deletion does not erase provider-held requests, recipient copies, exports, offline caches, device backups, or infrastructure logs. Destroying the active account file key blocks the reviewed service from decrypting files protected by that key. It does not erase plaintext legacy files or temporary copies, and cannot make backups unreadable if a backup also retains a recoverable account key and the server master key. Backup and log retention intervals are not established by the reviewed code, and the operator must confirm them before publication; no fixed deletion deadline is promised here.

You may review notes and transcripts, export available content, update class assignments and classes, and delete notes. You can ask for access to your personal information and its uses and disclosures, correction, deletion, or withdrawal of consent. The operator may verify your identity proportionately, explain any lawful refusal, and respond within applicable legal deadlines. Withdrawal may prevent the service from performing features requiring your information.

Applicable rights may arise under British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act or Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act, depending on the activity. This notice does not limit those rights. You may raise concerns with the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for British Columbia at https://www.oipc.bc.ca or the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada at https://www.priv.gc.ca.

Only submit other people's recordings or information with appropriate permission. Notes is not intended for children under 13. Parents or guardians can request help with a child's information at lz@kjt.lol. Access restrictions and account authentication reduce risk, but no online service can guarantee complete security.

Material policy changes will be announced before taking effect. New uses requiring consent will be presented separately. Contact lz@kjt.lol with questions.
